F.1 Weighted summation by parts

Use the grid and inner product \(\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_\ell\) of Proposition 11.4. Extend the grid vectors \(u,v\) by zero at the Dirichlet nodes. Let \(L_n=(M_n-I)/\Delta t\), let \(V^n\) be the sampled Robin coefficient, and write \(\delta_e u\) for the endpoint difference on edge \(e\), with a common orientation for both vectors. Then

\[\begin{aligned} \langle L_nu,v\rangle_\ell &=-\ell\sum_{e\ {\rm horizontal}}\omega_{k(e)}\,\delta_eu\,\delta_ev -\ell\sum_{e\ {\rm vertical}}\delta_eu\,\delta_ev\\ &\quad+\ell^2\sum_{i,j} V^n_{ij}u_{ij0}v_{ij0}, \qquad \omega_0=\tfrac12,\quad\omega_k=1\ (k>0). \end{aligned}\]
Derivation of the edge identity

Count each edge once, including those meeting a Dirichlet face. The contributions from its endpoints combine into the negative product of differences. In the vertical term at the lower face, the half-weight cancels the doubled normal stencil coefficient, giving the same edge weight as in the interior. In the Robin term, the bottom-node factor \(\ell^3/2\) multiplies the ghost-point contribution \(2V^n_{ij}u_{ij0}/\ell\), giving \(\ell^2 V^n_{ij}u_{ij0}\) in the pairing.

Taking \(v=u\) gives the energy pairing; taking \(v=1\) on active nodes and zero on Dirichlet nodes gives the mass balance and outer-face loss.

F.2 Consistency, stability and rates

For a fixed regularized coefficient and finite box, the convergence estimate requires separate consistency and stability hypotheses. Taylor expansion uses bounded time derivatives through order two and spatial derivatives through order four on the closed active region; the boundary stencil has its own residual estimate. Stability is a bound on the propagation of these residuals under the update. Neither a stability estimate nor the observed magnitude of the residual supplies the required regularity of the exact solution.

Separate spatial and temporal discretization from boundary truncation, coefficient regularization and Monte Carlo sampling. If \(E(h)\) is an error against a fixed reference and \(E(h)\sim Ch^p\), with the time error controlled along the refinement sequence, then

\[p_{\rm emp}(h)=\frac{\log(E(h)/E(h/2))}{\log 2}\longrightarrow p.\]

Self-convergence uses differences on common nodes in place of errors against an exact solution. It measures mesh sensitivity; the rate from such differences is not a certified error bound. At fixed time step, a spatial refinement can become limited by the temporal error.

F.3 Reproducibility

See the stability and convergence proofs, the discrete budget tests and the reproduction workflow. The computations use IEEE float64; display slices are rounded to seven decimals after diagnostics are calculated. Numerical curves are precomputed unless identified as analytical control demonstrations.
