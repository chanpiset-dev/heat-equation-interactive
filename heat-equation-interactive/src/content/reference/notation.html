Notation correspondence: the half-space is \(\mathcal H\) (as in the typeset research paper); the paper’s \(H_\alpha\) is written \(\Phi_\alpha\) here. The paper’s exponent loss in Theorem 1.3 is denoted by \(\eta\). The Sobolev symbol \(H^1\) is unchanged. Symbols confined to a calculation are defined locally.

Conventions: \(\mathscr X=H^1(\mathcal H),Y=L^2(\mathcal H)\) in the variational evolution (locally written \(X,Y\) in Chapter 13). In spectral theory, the ambient Hilbert space may be denoted \(\mathscr X\) locally; its operator is \(A_V\) when explicitly defined as self-adjoint, not the time-dependent boundary integral operator. The context specifies the meaning. \(C_N(\alpha)\) always denotes the characteristic coefficient; generic constants such as \(C(N)\) can change between estimates. \(L\) is a displacement bound or a local logarithmic cutoff width, whereas \(L_s\) is the stochastic regulator. The exponent loss \(\eta\) is distinct from regularization length \(\varepsilon\). Symbols introduced only inside individual proofs retain their local definitions.
