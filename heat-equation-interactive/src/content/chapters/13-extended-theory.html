Part V

13. Extended theory and remaining research questions

Which aspects of the theory are now established, and which require new mathematics?

Prerequisites and proof outline

Weighted regimes, critical weighted energy, and weak/integral identification.

Motion → profiles and uniqueness → admissible data → stability → multiple poles → geometry. In each topic separate established facts, missing estimates and conditional strategies.

The sharp Kato inequality determines the energy threshold, but it does not determine every aspect of the singular solution. The results of Chapter 3 and the extensions in Chapter 12 leave a number of questions about motion, asymptotics, uniqueness, data, and geometry. This chapter describes the frontier relative to the revised research paper and thesis and the supplementary results proved in this exposition. It is not a systematic survey of the current literature or a claim of priority. The questions and conjectural estimates below are not established theorems.

13.1 Rough supercritical trajectories

Reading convention. Numbered propositions and theorems with proofs are established in this exposition under their hypotheses. Statements called questions, conjectures or conditional strategies identify missing mathematics. The text does not claim that these questions are open throughout the literature.

For compact data and a continuous trajectory, Theorem 3.1 covers \(0<\lambda\le\lambda_N\); at \(\lambda=0\) the solution is \(P_tu_0\). For \(\lambda>\lambda_N\), Theorem 3.2 rules out finite solutions when \(\lim_{h\downarrow0}\omega(h)=0\). Chapter 12 gives further sufficient tests based on the small-scale modulus and locally regular intervals. Failure of a sufficient test leaves existence open.

Open question 13.1 · Classification beyond the displacement criteria

For constant \(\lambda>\lambda_N\), characterize those \(\xi\in C([0,T];\partial\mathcal H)\) for which the positive integral series is finite on \(D_\xi\) and defines an integral solution. In particular, does there exist a continuous trajectory with no locally certified supercritical episode for which a finite solution exists for a nontrivial compact datum?

Example (proved by Chapter 12). The path \(\xi(t)=t^\gamma e_1\), \(0<\gamma\le1/2\), fails (M) on the full interval since \(|\xi(h)-\xi(0)|/\sqrt h=h^{\gamma-1/2}\). Yet on any interval \([a,b]\subset(0,T]\) with \(a<b\) its derivative is bounded. Therefore its restriction satisfies (M), and Corollary 12.8a rules out a finite supercritical solution on any positive-length interval starting at zero. A rough initial cusp is not enough to evade nonexistence.

Research strategy: parabolic tangent trajectories

For \(t_0\in(0,T)\) define \(\zeta_r(s)=[\xi(t_0+r^2s)-\xi(t_0)]/r\) on the rescaled time interval. Set \(U_r(X,s)=u(\xi(t_0)+rX,t_0+r^2s)\). Where the differential equations are justified, the rescaled equations are

\[\partial_s U_r=\Delta_X U_r,\qquad \partial_\nu U_r=\frac{\lambda U_r}{|X-\zeta_r(s)|}.\]

The same scale invariance follows from the Gaussian formula in the integral equation after a restart (the initial datum and time interval also rescale). Under (M), \(|\zeta_r(s)|\le\sqrt{|s|}\,\omega(r^2|s|)\), uniformly tending to zero on bounded rescaled intervals. Without (M), nonconstant or noncompact tangent families may occur. This is a geometric observation, not convergence of \(U_r\). A useful target is to obtain uniform estimates for the rescaled positive series or weighted heat kernels, and to understand whether tangent-path information controls the singular exponential moment of Proposition 10.3. An obstruction is that a single tangent limit need not control all scales and all times. Finite expected path action does not control its exponential.

Research design. Test candidates whose restriction to every nontrivial time interval fails the available local nonexistence certificates; a single isolated cusp is inadequate. For a proposed oscillatory series, first prove continuity and quantitative lower bounds on local normalized increments in every interval, then test whether the lower comparison is inconclusive. Neither this property nor existence of nonconstant tangents proves solvability. Separate compactness of tangent paths, compactness of normalized solutions, and a theorem linking all relevant scales to the positive series.

13.2 Critical singularity profiles

Distinguish the proved time-integrated weighted energy and stationary upper bound from the unresolved pointwise moving critical asymptotic. The formal radial solutions below are candidate mechanisms, not computed or established minimal-solution profiles.

Theorem 7.5 now proves a time-integrated critical weighted-gradient estimate for the minimal solution, even for continuous moving trajectories. It does not imply finite weighted energy at every time, or a precise expansion of the transformed profile. In particular the logarithmic radial obstruction applies to an exact radial logarithmic component at a time where the weighted energy is finite, not to every possible asymptotic correction.

At \(\lambda=\lambda_N\), Chapter 7 constructs a finite minimal solution away from the moving graph. The estimate is in \(L^2\) and retains critical form control, but it does not give a bound on the ordinary gradient independent of regularization. Nonattainment of the sharp constant explains why critical concentration is possible. Existence is not a proof of a particular singularity profile.

Open question 13.2 · Critical asymptotics

For a fixed pole the upper estimate follows from Theorem 13.8 below. Determine the missing lower estimate there, and then investigate both estimates for trajectories satisfying (M): does the minimal solution for compact data satisfy, for every \(0<\tau<T\),

\[c_\tau\Phi_{\alpha_*}(x-\xi(t))\le u(x,t)\le C_\tau\Phi_{\alpha_*}(x-\xi(t))\]

for \(\tau\le t\le T\) and \(0<|x-\xi(t)|<r_\tau\), with positive finite constants. If this fails, determine the correct asymptotic scale, including possible logarithmic factors. The displayed two-sided estimate remains a research target; only the stationary upper half is proved below.

The weighted measure at criticality scales as \(R^2\) (Corollary 12.11a). This gives a precise reason to examine logarithms in the transformed equation.

Proposition 13.1 · Radial ground-state operator (supplementary)

For a stationary pole, let \(0<\alpha<N-2\) and \(w(x)=F(|x|)\), with \(F\) twice differentiable on an annulus away from zero. Then

\[\Phi_\alpha^{-2}\operatorname{div}(\Phi_\alpha^2\nabla w)=F''(r)+\frac{N-1-2\alpha}{r}F'(r).\]

At \(\alpha=\alpha_*\), the radial stationary solutions of the transformed equation on an annulus have the form \(F(r)=A+B\log r\). They satisfy homogeneous Neumann conditions on its planar boundary away from the pole.

Proof and scope

Euler's homogeneity identity gives \(x\cdot\nabla\log\Phi_\alpha=-\alpha\). Expand the weighted divergence as \(\Delta w+2\nabla\log\Phi_\alpha\cdot\nabla w\), and insert the radial gradient and Laplacian. The critical radial operator becomes \(F''+r^{-1}F'\). Its zero equation is \((rF')'=0\). At the flat boundary, the radial normal derivative is zero because \(x_N=0\). This proves the local differential statement. In general \(F=A+B\log r\) is not a globally nonnegative profile; the annular calculation neither identifies a global form domain nor proves an integral solution or a logarithmic correction to the minimal solution.

Proposition 13.7 · Critical logarithmic energy obstruction (supplementary)

Let \(F(r)=A+B\log r\), and let \(c_*=\int_{\mathbb S^{N-1}_+}\Phi_*(\theta)^2\,d\theta\). On the half-annulus \(a<r<R\),

\[\int_{a<|x|<R,\ x\in\mathcal H}\Phi_*^2|\nabla F|^2dx=c_*B^2\log(R/a).\tag{13.1}\]

Thus the radial logarithmic branch has infinite local weighted gradient energy at zero unless \(B=0\).

Proof and what this excludes

Homogeneity of degree \(-\alpha_*\) gives the polar weight \(r^{N-1-2\alpha_*}=r\). The derivative is \(B/r\), so direct integration gives (13.1). The angular factor is positive and finite by the two-sided profile bound on the unit hemisphere. In the notation of Corollary 12.11a, \(c_*=2C_*\) since the weighted volume of a half-ball is \(c_*R^2/2\). The constant branch has zero gradient energy. This excludes a nonzero exact logarithmic branch from the local weighted energy class, but it does not establish that every critical integral solution lies in that class or that general nonradial corrections have this form.

Theorem 13.8 · Stationary harmonic-barrier solvability, including endpoint weights (supplementary)

Let the pole be fixed at zero, \(N\ge3\), \(0<\alpha<N-2\) and \(0\le\lambda\le C_N(\alpha)\). Assume that \(f\ge0\) is continuous on \(\overline{\mathcal H}\setminus\{0\}\) and \(f\le M\Phi_\alpha\) for finite M. For every finite \(T\) there is a minimal nonnegative integral solution continuous off the singular graph, with the prescribed initial values, satisfying

\[0\le u(x,t)\le M\Phi_\alpha(x),\qquad (x,t)\in D_0.\tag{13.2}\]

Minimality is among all nonnegative integral competitors, not only weighted ones. At a characteristic root the bound includes equality \(\lambda=C_N(\alpha)\). For compact continuous data at \(\lambda=\lambda_N\), it yields \(u\le M\Phi_*\) for the stationary minimal solution.

Proof · positive iteration without a strict contraction

Use the harmonic identity of Chapter 9, valid at nonsingular boundary and volume points for all exponents in the stated open range. Let \(F=P_tf\), \(A=A_{\lambda/|y|}\) and \(W=M\Phi_\alpha\) (independent of time). Positivity of the free kernel and the identity give

\[F+AW\le M P_t\Phi_\alpha+\frac{\lambda}{C_N(\alpha)}M(\Phi_\alpha-P_t\Phi_\alpha)\le W.\]

The last inequality uses nonnegativity of the harmonic boundary contribution and \(\lambda/C_N(\alpha)\le1\). Define \(S_j=\sum_{k=0}^j A^kF\), or start from zero and iterate \(F+A\cdot\). Induction gives \(0\le S_j\le S_{j+1}\le W\). The iterates are Borel by measurable parameter integration; assign arbitrary zero values on the deleted graph, which is null for boundary space-time integration. Their pointwise limit \(S\) is finite at each nonsingular observation, and monotone convergence yields \(S=F+AS\) with finite integrals there. At the initial time the integral vanishes and the value is \(f\) away from zero. The boundary density is bounded by \(\lambda M |y|^{-\alpha-1}\); the weighted heat-potential continuity lemma 9.2 proves joint continuity up to the nonsingular boundary and both time endpoints, including the initial trace. The extended boundary supersolution argument of §9.7 (free term continuous for weighted data, Fatou for the nonnegative source) applies to every nonnegative competitor even if it lacks a weight bound. Induction bounds that competitor below by every \(S_j\), hence by \(S\). This proves minimality. For compact data, the positive profile dominates \(f\) by a constant on its compact support, since it is comparable to the positive power \(|x|^{-\alpha}\). At criticality the Chapter 7 construction is also minimal, so they coincide. The proof establishes existence and a time-uniform barrier, not endpoint uniqueness, a lower estimate, or moving endpoint-weight solvability.

Research strategy: identify the correct weighted heat kernel

Construct a stationary semigroup for the closed form associated with \(\Phi_*^2dx\). One must specify the closure at the pole and show that the resulting evolution agrees with the minimal integral solution, rather than another extension. Establish two-sided kernel bounds in the weighted measure, with enough continuity at the singular point to evaluate the transformed solution. Then test whether analogous estimates persist for moving poles. Quadratic volume scaling alone does not establish a Poincaré inequality, a Harnack inequality, or any of these heat-kernel estimates.

13.3 Matching lower singularity estimates

For \(0<\lambda<\lambda_N\) and condition (M), Chapter 9 proves an upper bound with every positive exponent loss \(\eta\): \(u(x,t)\le C_\eta |x-\xi(t)|^{-\alpha_--\eta}\) for radii at most one. Neither the extended weight interval nor the sharper continuation constant gives the endpoint estimate by letting \(\eta\downarrow0\).

Conjectural estimate 13.3 · Lower bound (manuscript research target)

Under the preceding subcritical hypotheses, for every \(\eta>0\) and \(0<\tau<T\), seek \(c_{\eta,\tau}>0\) and \(r_{\eta,\tau}>0\) such that

\[u(x,t)\ge c_{\eta,\tau}|x-\xi(t)|^{-\alpha_-+\eta},\quad 0<|x-\xi(t)|<r_{\eta,\tau},\quad \tau\le t\le T.\]

It would imply a logarithmic singularity exponent of \(\alpha_-\) when combined with the known upper bounds. A stronger question is two-sided comparability to \(\Phi_{\alpha_-}\).

Conditional consequence and candidate proof mechanism

If both bounds hold, take logarithms and divide by \(\log(1/r)>0\). Constants disappear as \(r\downarrow0\) and the upper and lower limits lie within \(\eta\) of \(\alpha_-\), uniformly in the indicated times and directions. Sending \(\eta\downarrow0\) proves the conditional log-exponent conclusion, not the lower estimate itself.

For a smooth moving trajectory set \(U(x,t)=u(x+\xi(t),t)\) and \(w=U/\Phi_\alpha\). The product rule yields

\[w_t=\Phi_\alpha^{-2}\operatorname{div}(\Phi_\alpha^2\nabla w)+\dot\xi\cdot\nabla w+(\dot\xi\cdot\nabla\log\Phi_\alpha)w.\]

At a matching root the planar condition is homogeneous Neumann. On parabolic scales a bounded velocity has rescaled size \(r|\dot\xi|\), but the coefficient involving \(\nabla\log\Phi_\alpha\) is singular. For merely continuous trajectories derivatives need not exist: work instead with the moving kernel. One route is a lower kernel comparison on short windows with a slightly smaller coupling and a compact positive restart datum, followed by a stationary lower-profile estimate for that smaller coupling. Establishing that stationary estimate and uniform positivity of the constants is a necessary missing step. A lower comparison alone is not a profile theorem.

Conditional transfer with uniform restart data

Suppose that for every \(0<\mu<\lambda_N\) and every nonzero compact nonnegative continuous datum \(g\), the stationary minimal solution satisfies a lower estimate of the form \(v_{\mu,g}(X,H)\ge c|X|^{-\alpha_-(\mu)}\) near zero at each fixed \(H>0\). For a given \(\eta>0\), select \(\mu<\lambda\) with \(\alpha_-(\mu)>\alpha_-(\lambda)-\eta\) by continuity of the smaller root. Choose \(\delta>0\) and a single window \(h\) with \(0<h<\tau/2\) so that the Chapter 8 lower comparison has effective coupling \(\mu_-\ge\mu\); condition (M) allows this uniformly over all windows of length \(h\). At each observation time \(t\) in [\(\tau\),T], restart at \(a=t-h\). Positivity gives \(u(X+\xi(a),a)\ge P_af(X+\xi(a))\). On a fixed compact half-ball strictly inside \(\mathcal H\), the latter has a uniform positive minimum because \(a\in[\tau/2,T]\), the centers stay in a compact set, and the free evolution is continuous and strictly positive. Choose a single nonzero smooth compact bump \(g\) below this minimum. The restart identity and boundary supersolution inequality give positive iteration on each window; lower kernel comparison and monotonicity in coupling then give \(u(X+\xi(t),t)\ge c_-v_{\mu,g}(X,D_-h)\). The hypothetical stationary estimate at this fixed time supplies a uniform \(c\) and radius, independent of \(t\). For \(r\le 1\) the stronger power implies the desired exponent loss. This reduces the moving lower estimate to the stated stationary input; it does not prove that input.

13.4 Uniqueness beyond harmonic weights

See Theorem 7.4 for strict-subcritical identification, distinct from unrestricted integral uniqueness. Critical energy-class uniqueness remains conditional on membership.

Chapter 7 proves minimality, and Theorem 9.1 gives uniqueness in each specified global harmonic-weighted class at strict subcriticality. It is not enough that a competing solution is continuous away from the pole. Neither compact initial data nor the bound for the minimal solution transfers automatically to all competitors.

Open question 13.4 · Zero-data solutions

In the solvable regimes, does the homogeneous integral equation \(v=A_\xi v\) admit a nonzero nonnegative finite solution, continuous on \(D_\xi\) with zero datum at every nonsingular initial point? Investigate the answer with and without additional growth, energy or harmonic-weight hypotheses. The equation must hold with finite right side at interior observations, as in Chapter 2.

Proposition 13.2 · Equivalent obstruction to uniqueness (supplementary)

For fixed nonnegative data with a finite minimal integral solution \(u_{\min}\), uniqueness among nonnegative integral solutions is equivalent to absence of a nonzero nonnegative zero-data integral solution in the same unrestricted class.

Proof

If \(u\) is another solution, minimality gives \(u\ge u_{\min}\) on \(D_\xi\). Subtraction of their finite interior integral identities gives \(v=u-u_{\min}=A_\xi v\), since the integrands are integrable at the observation and the difference is nonnegative. Continuity and the zero nonsingular initial trace follow by subtraction. Conversely, adding a nonnegative zero-data solution to \(u_{\min}\) preserves the integral equation, finiteness, trace and continuity. If it is nonzero, uniqueness fails. No bound at infinity or at the graph is inferred.

A possible strategy is to prove a posteriori bounds for arbitrary competitors, or to study the singular graph as a removable set for the zero-data equation under an appropriate energy condition. In a general integral class even finiteness of the global \(L^2\) norm is not known. Different formal ground states or a nonattained infimum do not construct a zero-data solution. At criticality the loss of strict contraction is a separate obstacle.

Proposition 13.9 · Uniqueness in the weak energy class (supplementary)

Let \(0\le\lambda\le\lambda_N\), \(\xi\) continuous, and \(f\in L^2(\mathcal H)\). There is at most one weak solution in \(\mathcal W=L^2(0,T;H^1(\mathcal H))\cap H^1(0,T;(H^1)^*)\) with \(L^2\) initial trace \(f\), satisfying the singular Robin form equation for almost every time. This is a uniqueness statement, not critical energy-class existence.

Proof

Use the pivot embedding of \(L^2\) into the dual of \(H^1\). Kato and Cauchy–Schwarz bound the boundary bilinear form uniformly by \((\lambda/\lambda_N)\|\nabla v\|_2\|\nabla w\|_2\). For the difference \(v\), the Hilbert-triple chain rule (B.2) makes testing by \(v\) legitimate and gives

\[\tfrac12\frac{d}{dt}\|v\|_2^2=-\mathcal E[v]+\int_{\partial\mathcal H}\frac{\lambda|\operatorname{Tr}v|^2}{|y-\xi(t)|}d\sigma\le0.\]

Since the continuous \(L^2\) representative has zero initial value, integration forces \(v=0\). No strict coercivity is needed for uniqueness in this class, but at equality the estimate alone does not guarantee membership of the minimal integral solution in \(\mathcal W\). Under strict subcriticality that membership is established in Theorem 7.4.

13.5 Admissible singular initial data

The weighted solvability results give sufficient data bounds, while the free heat term gives a necessary integrability obstruction. A proposed intermediate threshold is not a theorem.

Fix a stationary pole at zero and strict subcritical coupling. For a nonnegative cutoff power datum \(f(x)=\chi(x)|x|^{-k}\), let \(k\ge0\) and let the cutoff be continuous, compactly supported, equal to one near zero. The harmonic-weight theorem gives a sufficient range. The manuscripts suggest investigating a larger threshold \(k<N-\alpha_-\), motivated by the local integral of \(\Phi_{\alpha_-}f\).

Open question 13.5 · Sharp admissibility

For the stationary problem first, is local integrability of \(\Phi_{\alpha_-} f\), with an appropriate condition at infinity, sufficient and necessary for a finite minimal solution? For cutoff power data this suggests \(k<N-\alpha_-\). Determine the endpoint, possible logarithmic modifications, and whether the same characterization holds for slowly moving poles. This is a candidate threshold, not an established solvability criterion.

Proposition 13.3 · Elementary sufficient and necessary ranges (supplementary)

For the above power data, the extended weighted theorem proves existence for \(0\le k<\alpha_+\) under (M), also for a pole moving from zero. If \(k\ge N\), no finite nonnegative integral solution exists, even for arbitrary continuous motion. The interval between these bounds is not classified by these arguments.

Proof

Choose \(\alpha\in(\alpha_-,\alpha_+)\) with \(\alpha\ge k\), possible if \(k<\alpha_+\). By comparability of \(\Phi_\alpha\) with \(|x|^{-\alpha}\) and compactness of the cutoff support, \(f/\Phi_\alpha\) is bounded away from the pole and near it. Apply Theorem 9.1. For necessity, at any fixed positive-time interior observation the free kernel has a positive minimum on a sufficiently small closed half-ball about zero. The integral of \(r^{N-1-k}\) there diverges if \(k\ge N\). Since a nonnegative integral solution dominates its free term, finiteness is impossible.

The candidate weighted-integrability threshold is \(N-\alpha_- = \alpha_++2\) by root symmetry. It is strictly larger than the proved sufficient threshold and smaller than \(N\). This is why weighted integrability is not synonymous with membership in the pointwise weighted space. At \(k=\alpha_+\) the strict coefficient margin is lost; Theorem 13.8 supplies existence for a stationary pole, because the cutoff datum is dominated by \(\Phi_{\alpha_+}\). For moving poles this endpoint is not classified here.

Potential approach. In the stationary setting, a rigorously identified positive heat kernel with behavior comparable to \(\Phi_{\alpha_-}(x)\Phi_{\alpha_-}(y)\) for \(y\) near zero at a fixed positive time could connect integrability of the datum to finiteness of the evolution. This kernel estimate, continuity off the graph, and agreement with the positive series would all need proof. For moving poles, weighted-data Feynman–Kac (Theorem 10.4) provides an equivalent representation in its proved regime, not an extension by itself. The finite singular action proposition does not guarantee its exponential is integrable against a more singular datum.

13.6 Stability under changes of the trajectory

Chapter 12 proves locally uniform convergence for nonmonotone approximating coefficients dominated by a fixed singular coefficient, provided the limiting minimal solution is finite and continuous. If \(\xi_j\to\xi\) uniformly, the coefficients converge off the limiting graph but their poles are at different points. Uniform convergence of the paths does not imply domination of their potentials by the limiting potential. Even the meaning of locally uniform convergence requires avoiding the moving graphs of the approximants.

Open question 13.6 · Trajectory stability

For critical coupling \(\lambda=\lambda_N\) (strict subcriticality is proved below), and compact data, let \(\xi_j\to\xi\) uniformly on \([0,T]\). Do the corresponding minimal solutions converge locally uniformly on \(D_\xi\) (and in \(L^2\) at each time)? If not, identify sufficient additional trajectory or uniform integrability conditions. Compact subsets of \(D_\xi\) are disjoint from the approximating graphs for all sufficiently large indices.

Proposition 13.4 · A one-sided stability result (supplementary)

For fixed \(0\le\lambda\le\lambda_N\) and the uniformly converging continuous paths and compact data above, for any observations \((x_j,t_j)\to(x,t)\in D_\xi\) lying off the respective graphs, \(\liminf_j u_{\xi_j}(x_j,t_j)\ge u_\xi(x,t)\). In particular a limiting singular series that diverges at a positive-time interior observation forces divergence of the approximating positive series there; that latter statement does not require their finiteness.

Proof · Apply the unified series principle

Outside the countable union of the singular graphs, uniform path convergence gives pointwise coefficient convergence, hence almost-everywhere convergence on boundary space-time. By Proposition 2.2(3), the sequential lower bound holds for the extended series at every positive-time observation. The series is the minimal solution in the compact-data existence range by Chapter 7. At time zero use positivity and the free approximate identity; the divergent-series version follows directly from the extended lower bound. No upper inequality is asserted.

Proposition 13.5 · Fixed-regularization trajectory stability (supplementary)

For fixed \(\varepsilon>0\), fixed \(\lambda\ge0\), and bounded continuous nonnegative data, the bounded Robin solutions for two continuous trajectories obey \(\|u_{\xi,\varepsilon}-u_{\zeta,\varepsilon}\|_\infty\le C(\lambda,\varepsilon,T,\|f\|_\infty)\|\xi-\zeta\|_\infty\).

Proof

Both boundary coefficients are bounded by \(B=\lambda/\varepsilon\). Proposition 10.2's spatial Lipschitz constant \(K=2\lambda/(3\sqrt3\varepsilon^2)\) also bounds the variation under translation of the center, by the mean-value theorem. With \(E_B(T)=\sum_{k\ge0}B^k T^{k/2}/\Gamma(1+k/2)\), Lemma 7.1 gives \(\|u_{\zeta,\varepsilon}\|_\infty\le E_B(T)\|f\|_\infty\). Write \(d=u_{\xi,\varepsilon}-u_{\zeta,\varepsilon}\) and abbreviate the corresponding boundary operators by \(A_\xi,A_\zeta\) within this proof. Subtract the two equations and use the convergent inverse series:

\[d=(I-A_\xi)^{-1}(A_\xi-A_\zeta)u_{\zeta,\varepsilon}, \qquad \|(I-A_\xi)^{-1}\|\le E_B(T).\]

By the boundary kernel mass and the Lipschitz bound on the coefficients, \(\|A_\xi-A_\zeta\|\le K\|\xi-\zeta\|_\infty\int_0^T(\pi h)^{-1/2}\,dh\). Thus one may take \(C=2\sqrt{T/\pi}K E_B(T)^2\|f\|_\infty\). This is a fixed-positive-regularization estimate; its constant deteriorates as \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\).

Theorem 13.10 · Strong trajectory stability below criticality (supplementary)

Let \(0\le\lambda<\lambda_N\), let \(f\) be nonnegative compact continuous on the closed half-space, and let \(\xi_j,\xi\) be continuous boundary trajectories with \(\sup_t|\xi_j(t)-\xi(t)|\to0\). For the minimal integral solutions,

\[\sup_{0\le t\le T}\|u_{\xi_j}(t)-u_\xi(t)\|_2+\|u_{\xi_j}-u_\xi\|_{L^2(0,T;H^1)}\longrightarrow0.\tag{13.3}\]

They also converge uniformly on each compact subset \(K\) of \(D_\xi\), which lies in \(D_{\xi_j}\) for sufficiently large \(j\). No common Hölder modulus is required.

Proof · strong form convergence and weak identification

1. Forms and measurability. Write \(X=H^1(\mathcal H)\), \(Y=L^2(\mathcal H)\), \(c=1-\lambda/\lambda_N>0\), and \(b_a(v,w)=\int_{\partial\mathcal H}\lambda\operatorname{Tr}v\operatorname{Tr}w/|y-a|\,d\sigma\). The bilinear norm is bounded by \(\lambda/\lambda_N\) on \(X\) by Kato and Cauchy–Schwarz. For \(a_t=\xi(t)\), \(\mathfrak a_t=\mathcal E(\cdot,\cdot)-b_{a_t}\) is measurable on fixed pairs: truncate the potential, use measurable integration and absolute integrability (Kato); signed integrands follow by splitting positive and negative parts. The shifted form satisfies \(\mathfrak a_t(w,w)+c\|w\|_2^2\ge c\|w\|_X^2\). Appendix B's nonautonomous evolution theorem B.2 constructs a unique \(v\in\mathcal W\) with initial datum \(f\).

2. Strong convergence on fixed vectors. Let \(V_j,V\) be potentials with centers converging at a fixed time, and define \(R_jg=(b_j-b)(g,\cdot)\in X^*\). By Cauchy–Schwarz with the positive weight \(|V_j-V|\), then Kato,

\[\|R_jg\|_{X^*}^2\le\frac{2\lambda}{\lambda_N}\int_{\partial\mathcal H}|V_j-V|\,|\operatorname{Tr}g|^2d\sigma.\tag{13.4}\]

For a smooth compact \(g\) the last integral tends to zero: on the union of small radius-2r balls about the two centers, the integral of either inverse distance is bounded by \(C r^{N-2}\) for shifts smaller than \(r\); on the remainder of the fixed compact support the difference tends uniformly to zero (the denominators are bounded below by \(r\)). First let \(j\) tend to infinity and then \(r\) tend to zero. This also proves local \(L^1\) translation continuity without a differentiability assumption. Smooth functions compactly supported in the closed half-space are dense in \(X\). The uniform bound \(\|R_j\|_{X\to X^*}\le2\lambda/\lambda_N\) extends strong convergence to each fixed \(g\). The same reasoning holds for bounded regularizations of a fixed center: dominated convergence of \(\int |V_n-V||\operatorname{Tr}g|^2\) follows directly from Kato. Measurability of \(R_j(t)v(t)\) follows by the separability/simple-function argument in Appendix B. For \(v\in L^2(0,T;X)\), dominated convergence with the uniform norm bound now gives \(\|R_j(\cdot)v(\cdot)\|_{L^2(0,T;X^*)}\to0\). This is strong convergence on fixed vectors, not norm convergence of the form operators.

3. Energy comparison. For any of these approximating forms let \(v_j\) denote its weak solution, and \(e=v_j-v\). The weak difference equation is \(e_t+\mathfrak a_{j,t}(e,\cdot)=R_j(t)v\), with zero initial data. With \(r_j(t)=\|R_j(t)v(t)\|_{X^*}\), the chain rule and Young's inequality give

\[\tfrac12(\|e\|_2^2)' + c\|\nabla e\|_2^2\le r_j\|e\|_X\le\tfrac c2\|\nabla e\|_2^2+\tfrac12\|e\|_2^2+(\tfrac1{2c}+\tfrac12)r_j^2.\]

Here use \(\|e\|_X\le\|\nabla e\|_2+\|e\|_2\). The integrating factor \(e^{-t}\) gives a uniform-in-time bound on \(\|e\|_2^2\) by \(C\int_0^T r_j^2\); integrating the inequality also bounds \(\int_0^T\|\nabla e\|_2^2\) by a constant times that integral. Thus \(e\) tends to zero in both asserted norms. Constants depend on \(c\),T, not \(j\).

4. Identify the singular weak solution. Apply Theorem 7.4 separately to the limiting path and every approximating path. This identifies each weak solution in the common energy class with its minimal integral representative, including equality in \(Y\) at every time. Step 3 with the converging centers then proves (13.3). The subsequent local equicontinuity argument upgrades the convergence away from the graph.

Proof · locally uniform convergence up to the nonsingular boundary

Fix a point of \(K\). By continuity of the limiting path choose nested space-time cylinders with closed spatial supports and time interval at positive distance from its graph. For large \(j\) the same cylinders remain uniformly separated from every graph of \(\xi_j\). All bounded approximants for these \(j\) have their coefficients bounded there by a single constant, and all obey \(\sup_t\|u_{j,n}(t)\|_2\le\|f\|_2\) by Chapter 7. The local boundary/interior Moser estimates of §§7.4–7.5 give a uniform supremum bound on a smaller cylinder, with the constant extension by \(\|f\|_\infty\) for cylinders at time zero; the constants depend on cylinder geometry, local coefficient bound and \(f\), not on \(j\),n. Use a fixed spatial cutoff supported in this smaller cylinder and equal to one around the observation region. In (7.19) the boundary density is uniformly bounded on fixed compact support. Its recent-time integral is \(O(\sqrt\delta)\); the volume cutoff derivatives are separated from the observation and their recent-time bound is \(C\int_0^\delta(h^{-N/2}+h^{-(N+1)/2})e^{-c_0/h}dh\to0\). For elapsed times ≥\(\delta\) the kernels and their first spatial derivatives are uniformly continuous and bounded on the fixed compact ranges, and the time indicator changes on a set of length at most the observation-time difference. The free term from a fixed earlier slice is equicontinuous when elapsed time stays positive; at zero it is the common approximate identity applied to the cutoff datum. These are precisely the estimates of §7.6, with all constants now independent of both \(j\) and n. Pass \(n\to\infty\) pointwise: the common local bound and modulus survive for the singular minimal solutions. A finite cylinder cover gives local compactness on each such patch, including \(t=0,T\) and boundary patches. By Arzelà–Ascoli any subsequence on a closed smaller patch admits a uniformly converging subsequence. Its continuous limit equals \(u_\xi\) at every point of the patch: on the interior space-time portion this follows from strong space-time \(L^2\) convergence, and equality extends to its closure by continuity (take patches which are closures of nonempty relative cylinders). At the boundary and time endpoints this argument uses continuity from the interior, not a volume trace alone. If uniform convergence on \(K\) failed, a sequence of offending points would accumulate in one of these patches, contradicting the subsequential equality. This proves local uniform convergence. At criticality \(c=0\) and the energy comparison does not close; no critical stability claim is made.

Remaining critical upper-bound problem. At critical coupling, to pass to singular limits uniformly, one needs uniform control of the positive-series tails or the boundary density near the moving graphs. One possible program is to show uniform local estimates with control of the entire near-pole integral, then prove an identification of subsequential limits as the minimal solution. Neither mere boundedness of the global \(L^2\) norm nor fixed-regularization stability gives this conclusion.

13.7 Multiple moving singularities

There are two proved regimes: the sum condition permits collisions; uniform separation permits individually strictly subcritical couplings even when their sum exceeds the threshold. A snapshot of separated poles does not establish uniform separation over time.

Consider finitely many continuous boundary centers \(\xi_i\) and nonnegative constants \(\lambda_i\). Replace the coefficient by

\[V(y,t)=\sum_{i=1}^J\frac{\lambda_i}{|y-\xi_i(t)|}.\]

Define the nonsingular domain by deleting the union of their graphs. There are two fundamentally different cases: uniformly separated centers and centers that approach or collide. A collision at which several centers agree throughout an interval acts as a single pole with combined coupling on that interval. An instantaneous collision is not the same condition.

Proposition 13.6 · A conservative multi-pole existence criterion (supplementary)

If \(\sum_i\lambda_i\le\lambda_N\), the compact-data minimal solution exists on the complement of the union of the graphs for continuous trajectories, including collisions.

Proof by the construction of Chapter 7

Take bounded increasing approximations to each inverse-distance term. At each time, apply the translated Kato inequality to each term, even if two centers coincide, to obtain

\[\int_{\partial\mathcal H}V_n |\operatorname{Tr}w|^2\,d\sigma\le\frac{\sum_i\lambda_i}{\lambda_N}\mathcal E[w].\]

The bounded Robin construction and uniform energy estimate therefore give the same global \(L^2\) bound as in Chapter 7. A compact observation set off the finite union of continuous graphs has positive separation from that union. In sufficiently small space-time cylinders about its points, every bounded coefficient is uniformly bounded by a sum of constants; the boundary Moser and localization arguments of §§7.4–7.6 apply unchanged. At time zero use the constant extension from §7.5. Monotonicity and local equicontinuity yield a finite continuous limit off the union, including nonsingular endpoints. Monotone convergence in the kernel equation gives equality with finite right side at each such positive-time observation; Fatou and comparison with finite iterates prove minimality. The strong initial \(L^2\) trace follows by the same free-term comparison as §7.7. No separation or differentiability is used. This sufficient condition does not claim sharpness for separated centers.

Theorem 13.11 · Uniformly separated subcritical moving poles (supplementary)

Assume \(N\ge 3\), \(J\) finite, continuous boundary paths and nonnegative couplings with \(\max_i\lambda_i<\lambda_N\). Suppose \(d=\inf_{t\in[0,T]}\min_{i\ne j}|\xi_i(t)-\xi_j(t)|>0\) (for \(J=1\) use Chapter 7). For nonnegative compact continuous f, a finite minimal integral solution for the summed coefficient exists on the complement of the union of the singular graphs, with strong initial \(L^2\) trace and global \(L^2(0,T;H^1)\) control. The sum of the couplings is not required to be subcritical.

Proof · localized Kato inequality and construction

1. Partition at each time. Choose \(r=d/5\) and a smooth radial function \(\vartheta:[0,\infty)\to[0,\pi/2]\) equal to \(\pi/2\) on \([0,r]\), zero on \([2r,\infty)\), with bounded derivative. Put \(\chi_i(x,t)=\sin\vartheta(|x-\xi_i(t)|)\) for \(i\ge 1\). The supports are disjoint because 4r<d. Put \(\chi_0=\cos\vartheta(|x-\xi_i(t)|)\) on each radius-2r ball and 1 outside their union. These smooth spatial functions satisfy \(\sum_{i=0}^J\chi_i^2=1\) and \(\sum_i|\nabla\chi_i|^2\le C_r\) independently of time. They are flat near centers and smooth across support boundaries. The IMS identity follows by expanding \(\sum\mathcal E[\chi_i w]\) and using \(\sum\chi_i\nabla\chi_i=0\): it equals \(\mathcal E[w]+\int |w|^2\sum|\nabla\chi_i|^2\). No time differentiation of cutoffs is used.

2. Isolate the unbounded part. In the support of \(\chi_i\), every other center is at distance at least \(d-2r\). In the support of \(\chi_0\) all centers are at distance at least \(r\) (where \(\chi_0\) is nonzero). Thus for \(\theta_0=\max_i\lambda_i/\lambda_N<1\) and \(B_0=(\sum_i\lambda_i)/r\),

\[\begin{aligned}\int_{\partial\mathcal H}V|\operatorname{Tr}w|^2d\sigma&\le\sum_{i=1}^J\frac{\lambda_i}{\lambda_N}\mathcal E[\chi_i w]+B_0\|\operatorname{Tr}w\|_2^2\\&\le\theta_0\mathcal E[w]+\theta_0C_r\|w\|_2^2+B_0\|\operatorname{Tr}w\|_2^2.\end{aligned}\]

The trace inequality \(\|\operatorname{Tr}w\|_2^2\le2\|w\|_2\|\partial_Nw\|_2\le\eta\mathcal E[w]+\eta^{-1}\|w\|_2^2\) follows by integrating the normal derivative of \(w^2\) and density. Choose \(\eta\) positive with \(B_0\eta\le(1-\theta_0)/2\) (if \(B_0=0\) take \(\eta\)=1). Then

\[\int_{\partial\mathcal H}V|\operatorname{Tr}w|^2\le\theta\mathcal E[w]+C_0\|w\|_2^2,\quad\theta=\tfrac{1+\theta_0}{2}<1,\quad C_0=\theta_0C_r+B_0/\eta.\tag{13.5}\]

All constants are uniform in \(t\) and also apply to any pointwise smaller regularized potential. Individual critical couplings leave no margin in this estimate.

3. Approximate and pass to the limit. Take the sum of bounded increasing continuous regularizations of the poles. The bounded Robin construction of §7.1 gives nonnegative \(u_n\), increasing in \(n\); §7.2 gives the energy identity. From (13.5),

\[\tfrac12(\|u_n\|_2^2)' +(1-\theta)\mathcal E[u_n]\le C_0\|u_n\|_2^2.\]

Multiplication by \(e^{-2C_0t}\) and integration bounds the supremum of the volume norm and the time-integrated gradient, uniformly in \(n\). Every nonsingular observation has nested cylinders separated from the finite union of graphs, on which the entire coefficient is uniformly bounded. Apply the Moser and localization estimates of Chapter 7 (including the time-zero extension) with the new uniform \(L^2\) bound. The monotone limit is therefore finite and locally uniform off the union, with continuous initial and terminal traces at nonsingular points. Monotone convergence in the integral equation identifies the finite limit; nonnegative Fatou extends the competitor inequality to nonsingular boundary points and endpoints, and positive iteration proves minimality. The uniform energy estimates pass to the limit by Fatou and weak lower semicontinuity: weak \(L^2\)(\(H^1\)) subsequential limits agree almost everywhere with the monotone limit (bounded \(L^2\) and dominated convergence by that limit also give strong \(L^2\) in space-time). Finally \(u(t)\ge P_tf\) and \(\|u(t)\|_2^2\le e^{2C_0t}\|f\|_2^2\) imply \(\|u(t)-P_tf\|_2^2\le e^{2C_0t}\|f\|_2^2-\|P_tf\|_2^2\to0\), proving the strong initial trace. This completes existence without assuming differentiability of the paths.

Open question 13.7 · Sharp thresholds for separated and colliding poles

For uniformly separated centers, strict individual subcriticality is now sufficient by Theorem 13.11. Determine the sharp condition including critical poles. When separations vanish, characterize the effect of relative approach rates and durations of clustering. For example, is the pointwise condition \(\max_i\lambda_i\le\lambda_N\) sufficient in the uniformly separated case? Treat inclusion of individual critical couplings as a conjectural extension, not a consequence of Theorem 13.11.

One obstruction is already known: if one pole is supercritical and satisfies the local lower comparison on a nontrivial interval, the total potential dominates that single-pole potential. The restart argument of Chapter 12 then rules out finite solutions, since adding nonnegative terms only increases positive iterates. The same conclusion holds when a group of poles coincide throughout an interval and its summed coupling satisfies the corresponding strict comparison criterion.

Research strategy: localization and interaction estimates

When centers remain separated, a partition of unity with bounded derivatives can isolate neighborhoods of each pole. The other coefficients are bounded in each such neighborhood. The identity \(\sum_i|\nabla(\chi_i w)|^2=|\nabla w|^2+|w|^2\sum_i|\nabla\chi_i|^2\) for a smooth square partition \(\sum_i\chi_i^2=1\) follows by expanding and differentiating the partition identity. Apply the sharp trace inequality in each localized part and estimate the bounded remaining boundary terms by the elementary trace estimate of Appendix B. Strict individual subcriticality leaves a gradient margin that may absorb these errors. At criticality the bounded errors and their absorption require additional care. If the separation tends to zero, cutoff gradient bounds deteriorate. A proof for the critical separated regime and a scale-dependent estimate for collisions are distinct tasks.

13.8 Curved boundaries and geometric effects

For a smooth domain \(\Omega\subset\mathbb R^N\), a natural analogue is \(u_t=\Delta u\), \(\partial_\nu u=\lambda u/|x-\xi(t)|\) on \(\partial\Omega\), with a continuous trajectory in that boundary. Use Euclidean distance in the coefficient unless a different metric is explicitly specified. The half-space Neumann kernel and the exact translated trace inequality are no longer available.

Open question 13.8 · Local geometry and the sharp threshold

For a sufficiently smooth boundary, determine whether the flat constant \(\lambda_N\) governs the local existence/nonexistence threshold, and what global lower-order geometric terms affect critical solvability. Identify the relevant assumptions on bounded or unbounded domains, chart uniformity, trajectory regularity and initial data. A local tangent-plane calculation alone does not settle critical existence.

At a boundary point with horizontal tangent plane, write the surface locally as \(z=F(x')\), \(F(0)=0\), \(\nabla F(0)=0\). Under the scale \(x=rX\) the graph becomes \(Z=F(rX')/r\). If the Hessian is bounded then Taylor's formula gives \(|F(rX')|/r\le C r|X'|^2\) locally. Thus the rescaled geometry tends to a plane on bounded sets. This elementary geometric statement does not imply convergence of kernels or solutions.

Research strategy: perturb the flat estimates

Flatten the boundary in a chart. The bulk energy becomes a uniformly elliptic variable-coefficient form, the boundary measure acquires a Jacobian, and the distance factor becomes a perturbed radial weight. At small scales these coefficients approach their flat values. Seek a localized trace inequality with an arbitrarily small leading-constant error and controlled lower-order remainder. Such an error can be absorbed below critical coupling but not at equality. For supercritical nonexistence one would need localized negative test functions, a bounded-approximation spectral argument (or a suitable time-dependent replacement), and lower heat-kernel comparison. Proving the analogue of the critical local estimates and the correct global solution class is a separate challenge. No general curved-domain theorem is asserted here.

Three distinct geometric targets
Strict subcriticalityFlatten charts and prove a uniform localized trace inequality with arbitrarily small leading error.
SupercriticalityConcentrate negative-form tests and establish local kernel lower bounds or a time-dependent substitute.
CriticalityControl the remainder at the exact leading constant; a positive relative error is not absorbable.
\[\lambda_N\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{|\operatorname{Tr}w|^2}{|y-a|}d\sigma\le(1+\eta)\int_\Omega|\nabla w|^2+C_\eta\|w\|_2^2.\]

This is a target inequality, not a proved curved-domain estimate here. Uniform chart size, bounded Jacobians, comparison of Euclidean distances, a global trace bound for the remainder, and kernel continuity at the boundary would all need to be supplied. Compact smooth boundaries are a natural first setting.

13.9 Synthesis and priorities for future research

The critical weighted-energy estimate is established in Theorem 7.5, the singular weak identification in Theorem 7.4, and stationary endpoint probabilistic representation in Corollary 10.5. The research frontier is sharp pointwise critical behavior, critical stability, lower estimates and unrestricted uniqueness, not existence of the integrated weighted-gradient bound.
