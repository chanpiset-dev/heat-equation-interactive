Part IV

11. Numerical investigations

What can a finite grid reliably tell us about the regularized PDE?

Prerequisites and proof outline

Regularization, heat balance, and Appendix F for numerical error and reproducibility.

Define the finite box → derive one update → positivity and stability → heat budgets → refinement and domain sensitivity → matched stochastic benchmark.

The numerical model uses a finite box and a bounded Robin coefficient. It illustrates diffusion and amplification but cannot establish a singular threshold. We first reproduce Chapter 9 of the revised thesis [5], then give supplementary stability and convergence arguments and a matched probabilistic benchmark.

11.1 The computational model

The theory uses an infinite half-space and a singular boundary coefficient; the experiments use a finite box, a bounded coefficient and discrete nodes. These changes are mathematically distinct: domain truncation, regularization and discretization. The resulting temperatures are not direct evaluations of a singular solution.

\[\begin{aligned}\Omega_R&=(-R,R)^2\times(0,R),\\ u_t&=\Delta u&&\text{in }\Omega_R,\\ -u_z&=V_\varepsilon u&&\text{on }\Gamma_0,\\ u&=0&&\text{on }\Gamma_D,\\ V_\varepsilon(x,y,t)&=\frac{\lambda}{\sqrt{(x-a(t))^2+y^2+\varepsilon^2}}.\end{aligned}\]

The bottom face is \(\Gamma_0\); the top and sides are \(\Gamma_D\). At their intersections the discrete Dirichlet value is zero. The original settings are \(R=2,\lambda=0.55,\varepsilon=0.20\), \(a(t)=0\) or \(1.5t\), and \(T=0.28\). The initial grid field is

\[u_0(x,y,z)=\exp\left(-\frac{(x-0.15)^2+y^2}{0.5^2}-\frac{(z-0.45)^2}{0.35^2}\right),\]

with outer Dirichlet nodes overwritten by zero. The grid is uniform, \(\Delta x=\Delta y=\Delta z=\ell=0.1\), and \(\Delta t=0.0007\) (400 steps). Both runs start with the same field. The moving center alters amplification, not advection.

Why the manuscript datum is not classically compatible at time zero

The unmodified bump satisfies \(\partial_z u_0(x,y,0)=2(0.45)u_0(x,y,0)/(0.35)^2>0\), whereas the Robin condition requires \(\partial_z u_0=-V_\varepsilon u_0\le0\). Moreover, the Gaussian is positive on the lateral and top faces; overwriting the grid values does not make its continuous restriction compatible. Thus a smooth solution with bounded derivatives all the way to time zero cannot be assumed for this experiment. The numerical reproduction is retained as specified. The conditional error estimates below require separate regularity and compatible data.

11.2 Finite differences and the Robin ghost point

Write \(u^n_{ijk}\) for the approximation at time \(n\Delta t\), and \(r=\Delta t/\ell^2\). The six neighbors of an interior node give the seven-point explicit update

\[u^{n+1}_{ijk}=(1-6r)u^n_{ijk}+r\sum_{q\sim(i,j,k)}u_q^n.\tag{11.1}\]

At a bottom node the centered normal derivative is \((u_{ij,1}-u_{ij,-1})/(2\ell)\). Since the outward normal is \(-e_z\), the ghost value satisfies

\[u_{ij,-1}=u_{ij,1}+2\ell V^n_{ij}u_{ij,0}.\]

Inserting this into the centered second derivative gives the exact discrete elimination,

\[u^{n+1}_{ij,0}=(1-6r+2\Delta t V^n_{ij}/\ell)u^n_{ij,0}+r\sum_{q\sim_{xy}(i,j,0)}u^n_q+2r u^n_{ij,1}.\tag{11.2}\]

11.3 Positivity, order and stability

Proposition 11.1 (Discrete comparison; manuscript argument). If \(0\le r\le1/6\) and \(V\ge0\), the updates have nonnegative coefficients. For nonnegative data, solutions stay nonnegative; if the datum and coefficient are ordered, the resulting arrays are ordered at every step.

Proof. Every off-diagonal weight in (11.1)–(11.2) is nonnegative and the center weight is at least \(1-6r\). Increasing \(V\) increases the center weight; induction gives the assertion. The interior row sum is at most one and the bottom row sum is at most \(1+2\Delta t B/\ell\), where \(B=\lambda/\varepsilon\). Hence \(\|u^n\|_\infty\le(1+2\Delta t B/\ell)^n\|u^0\|_\infty\). This finite-mesh bound deteriorates as \(\ell\downarrow0\).

Supplementary uniform stability and conditional convergence proof
Proposition 11.2 (Weighted maximum stability). Fix \(R,B\) and \(\ell\le\ell_0\), assume the positivity step restriction, and let \(0\le V\le B\). With \(w(z)=e^{-Bz}\), the homogeneous-Dirichlet update matrix \(M_n\) satisfies \(M_nw\le(1+C\Delta t)w\) on active nodes, for \(C=B^2\cosh(B\ell_0)\).

Proof. At interior nodes, the normal discrete Laplacian divided by \(w\) is \(2(\cosh(B\ell)-1)/\ell^2\le C\); the tangential terms cancel. At the bottom the ratio is \(2(e^{-B\ell}-1)/\ell^2+2V/\ell\le B^2\), by \(e^{-x}\le1-x+x^2/2\) for \(x\ge0\). Removing positive weights for Dirichlet nodes can only decrease these bounds. Since \(M_n\) is positive, \(\|M_nv\|_w\le(1+C\Delta t)\|v\|_w\), with \(\|v\|_w=\max |v_i|/w_i\). The product over steps is at most \(e^{Ct_n}\). Because \(e^{-BR}\le w\le1\), this is a mesh-uniform maximum-norm stability estimate at fixed regularization and box size.

Corollary (Conditional convergence). Suppose the exact regularized finite-box solution is sufficiently smooth up to every active grid node and time zero, with uniformly bounded time derivatives through order two and spatial derivatives through order four. Impose exact Dirichlet and initial grid data. Then the maximum error on \([0,T]\) is \(O(\Delta t+\ell)\) at fixed \(B,R\).

Taylor expansion gives residual per unit time \(O(\Delta t+\ell^2)\) at interior nodes. At the bottom, expand \(u(z+\ell)\) about zero. The term \(2u_z/\ell\) cancels \(2Vu/\ell\), leaving \(u_{zz}+\ell u_{zzz}/3+O(\ell^2)\). Thus the residual is \(O(\Delta t+\ell)\). The error recurrence has zero initial error and forcing \(\Delta t\) times this residual, with zero Dirichlet error. Iteration in the weighted norm gives at most \(Te^{CT}e^{BR}\) times the uniform residual bound. This proves the stated sufficient rate, not an optimal rate. The manuscript bump with imposed zero outer nodes need not satisfy compatibility conditions, so this smooth-solution estimate cannot be applied to it without additional regularity analysis. Constants are not uniform in \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) or \(R\to\infty\).

Supplementary proposition 11.3 — second-order spatial convergence by a strict boundary barrier
Proposition 11.3 (Supplementary, conditional). Under the smoothness, exact grid data and positivity assumptions of Proposition 11.2 and its corollary, at fixed \(B,R,T\), the maximum error over active grid nodes and all time levels is \(O(\Delta t+\ell^2)\).

Proof. Write \(e^n=u^n-U^n\), where \(U^n\) is the exact solution sampled on the grid. The error satisfies \(e^{n+1}=M_ne^n+\Delta t\,r^n\) with zero initial and Dirichlet errors. Taylor expansion gives \(|r_i^n|\le K(\Delta t+\ell^2)\) at interior nodes and \(|r_i^n|\le K(\Delta t+\ell)\) at bottom nodes. These constants are independent of the mesh by the assumed derivative bounds. Put \(\kappa=B+1\), \(w_i=e^{-\kappa z_i}\), \(m=e^{-\kappa R}\), and \(d=\Delta t+\ell^2\). For \(\ell\le\ell_0\), let \(C_0=\kappa^2\cosh(\kappa\ell_0)\). The argument of Proposition 11.2 gives

\[\frac{(M_n-I)w_i}{\Delta t\,w_i}\le\begin{cases}C_0,&z_i>0,\\ C_0-2(\kappa-B)/\ell,&z_i=0.\end{cases}\]

At the bottom this follows from \(2(e^{-\kappa\ell}-1)/\ell^2+2V/\ell\le\kappa^2-2(\kappa-B)/\ell\); missing Dirichlet neighbors only lower the row. Choose \(C=C_0+1\) and \(W_i^n=A d e^{Ct_n}w_i\) on active nodes (zero Dirichlet error). Using \(e^{C\Delta t}\ge1+C\Delta t\), we have

\[W_i^{n+1}-(M_n W^n)_i\ge A d e^{Ct_n}w_i\Delta t\begin{cases}1,&z_i>0,\\1+2(\kappa-B)/\ell,&z_i=0.\end{cases}\]

Since \(d/\ell\ge\ell\), the bracket absorbs the boundary residual; indeed \(d(1+2/\ell)\ge\Delta t+\ell\). Taking \(A\ge K/m\) dominates \(\Delta t|r_i^n|\) everywhere. Positivity implies \(|e^{n+1}|\le M_n|e^n|+\Delta t|r^n|\), so induction from zero yields \(|e_i^n|\le W_i^n\le A e^{CT}d\). This proves the claim. The strict normal slope gives a boundary margin of order \(1/\ell\); treating the boundary residual as a bulk forcing would lose that gain. \(\square\)

This is a sufficient maximum-norm theorem at fixed regularization and domain. Its constants depend on the assumed derivative bounds, \(B,R,T\), and need not be uniform in singular or infinite-domain limits. It is not a convergence theorem for the incompatible manuscript bump.

11.4 Peak, mass and energy diagnostics

For a nonnegative classical solution, integrating the PDE gives

\[M'(t)=\int_{\Gamma_0}V_\varepsilon u\,dS+\int_{\Gamma_D}\partial_\nu u\,dS,\qquad M(t)=\int_{\Omega_R}u\,dx.\]

The second integral is nonpositive. A local peak can fall even when mass increases. The thesis's original diagnostic uses the full-weight grid sum \(\ell^3\sum u_{ijk}\). Its refinement study instead uses the trapezoidal mass (half weight at the bottom, all other faces zero) and analogously weighted squared \(L^2\) norm. We retain both conventions explicitly.

Observed contrast in the original stationary run: the volume maximum changes from 0.9700 to 0.3320 while the trapezoidal mass changes from 0.4695 to 0.5957. Diffusion can lower a peak while amplification increases total heat.

Exact discrete heat and energy budgets
Proposition 11.4 (Supplementary exact discrete balances). Let \(\langle u,v\rangle_\ell=\ell^3\sum_{i,j,k}\omega_k u_{ijk}v_{ijk}\) over active nodes, with \(\omega_0=1/2\), \(\omega_k=1\) otherwise. Extend grid values by zero on the Dirichlet faces. Write \(L_n=(M_n-I)/\Delta t\), \(M_\ell^n=\langle u^n,1\rangle_\ell\), and \(E_\ell(u)=\ell\sum_{\text{horizontal edges}}\omega_k(\delta u)^2+\ell\sum_{\text{vertical edges}}(\delta u)^2\), where each edge is counted once, \(\delta u\) is the difference of its endpoint values, and edges ending at a Dirichlet node are included.
\[\frac{M_\ell^{n+1}-M_\ell^n}{\Delta t}=J_R^n-J_D^n,\quad J_R^n=\ell^2\sum_{ij}V^n_{ij}u^n_{ij0}.\]

Here \(J_D^n=\ell\sum_{\text{lateral face neighbors}}\omega_k u^n_{ijk}+\ell\sum_{\text{top face neighbors}}u^n_{ijk}\); a node adjacent to two faces contributes to both. For nonnegative arrays both fluxes are nonnegative. Each pair of interior edge contributions cancels in the weighted sum; at the bottom the doubled normal coefficient is canceled by the half weight, and the Robin term contributes exactly \(J_R^n\). At the outer faces only the negative contribution from the active node remains. This proves the mass identity, including the edge and corner bookkeeping.

\[\frac{\|u^{n+1}\|_\ell^2-\|u^n\|_\ell^2}{\Delta t}=-2E_\ell(u^n)+2\ell^2\sum_{ij}V^n_{ij}|u^n_{ij0}|^2+\Delta t\|L_nu^n\|_\ell^2.\]

For the energy identity, pair the stencil with \(u^n\). On each active edge the two terms combine to minus the squared difference, including the bottom vertical edge because of the doubled coefficient and half weight; a Dirichlet edge contributes minus the active squared value. The Robin term contributes the positive boundary square. Finally expand \(\|u^n+\Delta t L_nu^n\|_\ell^2\) exactly. The last term is the nonnegative explicit-Euler correction, not a continuous PDE source. Neither identity alone implies decay. \(\square\)

11.5 Coupling, regularization and trajectory sensitivity

The six coupling runs use \(\lambda/\lambda_3=1/2,1,2\), \(\varepsilon=0.1,0.2\), the linear motion, and the original mesh. The five trajectory runs fix \(\lambda=\lambda_3,\varepsilon=0.2\), comparing a stationary pole and \(a(t)=0.42(t/T)^p\), \(p=1,0.75,0.5,0.3\). These reproduce thesis Figures 9.5–9.6. Monotone coefficient ordering explains the coupling and regularization ordering, not the trajectory ordering. The smallest regularization equals the mesh spacing and is poorly resolved.

11.6 Spatial and temporal refinement

For the linear trajectory at \(\lambda=\lambda_3,\varepsilon=0.2\), compare four meshes. Changing only the time step probes temporal sensitivity; changing both the grid and time step probes their combined effects. None of these differences is a certified error bound.

To measure actual error, use a supplementary smooth normal-coordinate problem with constant Robin coefficient \(b=0.7\), initial data \(e^{-bz}\), and prescribed exact value at \(z=R=2\). The exact solution is \(u(z,t)=e^{b^2t-bz}\). It satisfies the heat equation and \(-u_z(0,t)=bu(0,t)\). This one-dimensional benchmark is an exact tangentially constant half-space model, with exact artificial boundary values for the discrete calculation; it is not the original 3D box.

11.7 Finite-domain effects

Keeping the positive regularization, trajectory, original spacing and step fixed, we enlarge \(R=1,2,3\). The bump is restricted to the respective boxes; outer nodes are zero. These are supplementary runs. The mass is integrated over different boxes and is not a norm of a difference between solutions. A common point \((0.4,0,0.1)\) also measures local sensitivity. For nested boxes, positivity and zero Dirichlet data imply comparison by restricting the larger discrete solution to the smaller grid (assuming aligned grids and identical parameters). This does not quantify the infinite-domain error.

11.8 Matched microscopic and macroscopic benchmark

Use the same constant coefficient and exponential datum as Figure 11.6. For the half-line reflected process at height \(z\), with \(X_t=z+\sqrt2 W_t+L_t\), the path contribution simplifies:

\[e^{bL_t}e^{-bX_t}=e^{-b(z+\sqrt2W_t)}.\tag{11.3}\]

The normal moment-generating function gives the exact expectation \(e^{b^2t-bz}\). In this supplementary example, the terminal regulator is sampled from the conditional bridge minimum (Chapter 10), though it cancels algebraically. The finite-interval discretization uses the exact half-line value at its top boundary, so both methods approximate the same solution at common locations. This is not a comparison between the killed finite box and an unbounded reflected path ensemble with mismatched data.

\[\operatorname{Var}(e^{bL_t-bX_t})=e^{-2bz+2b^2t}(e^{2b^2t}-1).\]

For the exponential benchmark the theoretical standard error is \(u(z,t)\sqrt{(e^{2b^2t}-1)/n}\). It is distinct from the empirical standard error. A more stringent test retains the regulator in the weight.

Supplementary benchmark (nontrivial local time). For constant datum one, the reflection principle yields \(L_t\overset{d}= (|Z|-z)_+\) with \(Z\sim\mathcal N(0,2t)\). Splitting at \(|Z|=z\) and completing the square gives
\[m_b(z,t)=\operatorname{erf}(z/(2\sqrt t))+e^{b^2t-bz}\operatorname{erfc}(z/(2\sqrt t)-b\sqrt t).\]

The variance of \(e^{bL_t}\) is \(m_{2b}-m_b^2\). For the deterministic comparison, start instead at \(t_0=0.1\) with datum \(m_b(z,t_0)\), impose the exact time-dependent top value, and evolve for \(T=0.28\). The same datum is used in the stochastic estimator \(e^{bL_T}m_b(X_T,t_0)\). By the time-homogeneous reflected Markov property and additive regulator, its expectation equals \(m_b(z,t_0+T)\). The regulator does not cancel. The positive-time profile is smooth and compatible; the theoretical variance formula above is for datum one, not the shifted datum. Brownian-bridge terminal sampling is exact in law for ideal random draws.

11.9 Reproducibility and the order of limits

There are three independent numerical questions: convergence of the scheme for fixed \(\varepsilon,R\); removal of the outer boundary; and the singular limit \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\). The first is conditional on regularity in Proposition 11.2 and tested empirically for the manuscript bump. The latter two require additional uniform estimates. A large value at small \(\varepsilon\) is not evidence of singular nonexistence; a bounded value is not evidence of existence. The mathematical threshold was established in Chapter 7 and Chapter 8.

Chapter 11 · Takeaways

Positive weights imply order, not decay. A weighted maximum estimate controls fixed-regularization stability. Smooth exact benchmarks measure discretization error; finite-box mesh studies measure sensitivity. None replaces the singular PDE proofs.
